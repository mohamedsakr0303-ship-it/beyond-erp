# Writing style

Every word on screen, in documents and in messages follows these rules, in English and in Arabic.

## Principles

- **Short.** One idea per sentence. Labels are nouns; buttons are verbs.
- **Exact.** Use the accounting term: post, void, reverse, voucher, journal entry, ledger, receivable, payable.
- **Neutral.** No chat, no jokes, no exclamation marks, no rhetorical questions.
- **No obvious advice.** Do not explain what a field or button plainly does ("edit freely", "type here", "you can").
- **One name for one thing.** The same term everywhere: menus, forms, reports, exports and messages.

## English

- Sentence case for labels, buttons and messages ("New journal entry", "Save and post").
- Screen and menu names keep their established capitals ("Statement of Account").
- Help text: one sentence, under about 20 words, saying what the setting does or why it matters.
- Messages state the result: "Saved.", "Posted.", "3 records deleted."
- Errors state what is wrong and, briefly, how to fix it: "The books are closed up to 31 Mar 2026. Change the lock date to post on this date."
- Avoid: you can, simply, just, please, feel free, the person, stuff, things, money (when amount is meant), etc.

## Arabic

- Modern Standard Arabic, formal accounting usage; no colloquial words.
- Established terms: قيد يومية، ترحيل، إلغاء، عكس القيد، سند قبض، سند صرف، دفتر الأستاذ، ميزان المراجعة، الذمم المدينة، الذمم الدائنة، وحدة الأعمال، الطرف.
- The Arabic carries the same meaning as the English, in natural Arabic word order, not a word-for-word copy.
- Buttons in the imperative or verbal noun, consistently within a screen (حفظ، ترحيل، إلغاء).

## Placeholders and examples

- Generic only: no brand, person, product or place names in defaults or examples.
